How to handle sundowning at home

The short answer

Sundowning is restlessness, confusion or distress that gets worse in the late afternoon and evening. It usually helps to keep the day active, the late afternoon calm and bright, cut down noise and caffeine, and answer the feeling behind the words rather than arguing with the facts.

Written by The Evenhome care teamClinical review pending: no article is published as medical guidance until a qualified reviewer signs it offLast updated 21 November 2026

What it looks like

From around 3 or 4 pm your mum may pace, ask to 'go home' while already at home, become suspicious, cry, or get angry with the person they love most. It is not deliberate, and it is not a sign you are doing something wrong.

Common triggers

  • Tiredness, too little or too much daytime sleep
  • Fading light and shadows that look like people
  • Hunger, thirst, pain, constipation or needing the toilet
  • A busy house: TV, visitors, cooking noise
  • Changes in routine, a new support worker or a recent hospital stay
A woman holding a cup of tea by a window

A calm late-afternoon routine

  • Get outside or near a window in the morning; daylight helps the body clock
  • Keep naps short and early
  • A snack and a drink at about 3 pm
  • Lights on before it gets dark; close curtains to cut reflections
  • One familiar, gentle task: folding towels, sorting buttons, watering plants
  • Their music from age 15 to 25, played quietly
  • Fewer visitors and no big decisions after 4 pm

What to say

Answer the feeling, not the fact. If they say 'I need to go home', try 'You miss home. Tell me about it', then offer a cup of tea or a walk around the garden. Correcting them ('you are home') usually makes it worse.

When to call the doctor

A sudden change in behaviour can be a sign of an infection, pain or a medicine side effect. Call their doctor if sundowning appears suddenly or gets much worse in a few days. Never change medicines without medical advice.

Every Evenhome client with dementia has a written sundowning plan, built with the family and followed by every support worker.
