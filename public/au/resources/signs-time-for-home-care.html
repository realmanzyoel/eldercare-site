Signs it is time for home care for dementia

It is time to bring in home care when safety, eating, hygiene or the health of the family carer start to slip: falls or near misses, weight loss, missed medicines, an unwashed or unchanged look, getting lost, or a carer who is exhausted. Starting a few hours a week before a crisis is easier than starting after one.

The short answer

It is time to bring in home care when safety, eating, hygiene or the health of the family carer start to slip: falls or near misses, weight loss, missed medicines, an unwashed or unchanged look, getting lost, or a carer who is exhausted. Starting a few hours a week before a crisis is easier than starting after one.

Ten signs families tell us they missed

  1. Unopened mail and unpaid bills
  2. Spoiled food in the fridge, or very little food
  3. Weight loss or looking thinner in the face
  4. The same clothes several days running; a change in personal hygiene
  5. Burnt pans or the stove left on
  6. Bruises they cannot explain
  7. Missed doses, or doses taken twice
  8. Getting lost on a familiar route
  9. Withdrawing from friends, church or clubs
  10. The family carer is not sleeping, is ill or is angry most of the time
An older couple reviewing paperwork at home

Why earlier is easier

Introducing a support worker while your mum can still enjoy the company and get to know them is much easier than after a fall or a hospital stay. A few hours a week of companionship now builds the trust you will need later for washing and dressing.

A quick self-check

If you said yes to three or more of the signs above, book a free assessment or try our 2-minute care needs quiz. It suggests starting hours and does not ask for your email first.

What an assessment looks at

  • Daily routine and what they still enjoy
  • Safety in the home: stairs, bathroom, kitchen, doors
  • Meals, drinks, medicines and sleep
  • Memory and mood, and what helps
  • Who helps now, and how they are coping
