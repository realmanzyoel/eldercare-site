Respite care

Take a break without worrying

Hourly, full-day and overnight breaks for family carers, from a trained support worker who already knows them.

Who it is for

For family carers who need time back.

You are looking after your mum, your dad or your partner, and you have not had a day off in months. You love them. You are also worn out.

Respite care means a trained support worker steps in for a few hours, a day or a night, so you can sleep, work, see friends or simply sit down.

Options

From a few hours to a weekend.

4-hour break

A morning to yourself. Great for appointments, a haircut or a nap.

Full day

Eight hours: go to work, visit friends, or do nothing at all.

Overnight

Sleep through. A support worker stays awake or sleeps over, as the plan needs.

Weekend or holiday

A team covers a few days so you can travel or attend a family event.

Caring for the carer is part of the care.

A rested carer is a kinder, safer carer. Breaks are not a luxury.

Caregiver burnout

Signs it is time for a break.

You are tired even after sleeping

Exhaustion that does not lift is one of the earliest signs. It is a signal to get help, not to try harder.

You feel resentful, then guilty

Very common and not a sign you love them less. It is a sign you are carrying too much alone.

You have stopped seeing friends

Isolation creeps up on carers. A regular break gives you one fixed afternoon that is yours.

Your own health is slipping

Missed check-ups, back pain, poor sleep. See your own doctor and tell them you are a carer.

You snap at the person you care for

It happens to good people under too much strain. Take a break before it gets worse, and talk to someone.

How we are different

A break you do not spend worrying.

Same face each time

Regular breaks use the same support worker, so they become a familiar friend.

Dementia-trained, every one

Every support worker finishes our dementia training before their first visit, not 'when they get to it'.

The same small team

Two or three support workers who know them, and a backup you meet before day one.

You can see it happen

The family app shows check-ins, care notes and a weekly summary.

Promises with numbers

Start times, replacement times and call-back times we measure and publish.

Questions

Five questions about respite care.

What is respite care?

Care that gives the usual family carer a break, from a few hours to an overnight or a weekend.

Will they be OK with someone new?

We introduce the support worker before the first break, use the Life Story profile and keep the same support worker for future breaks.

Can I book respite at short notice?

Yes when we have support workers free; booking a regular break is the most reliable way to get one.

Is there funding for respite?

Respite can be part of a Support at Home plan. Funding explained.

Do I have to leave the house?

No. Many carers stay home and sleep, or work in another room. The break is yours to use.
