The short answer

Before you sign, ask about licensing, screening, training, staffing, reliability, communication, prices and complaints. A good agency answers all 15 questions below plainly, in writing, and is happy to be checked.

Written by The Evenhome care teamClinical review pending: no article is published as medical guidance until a qualified reviewer signs it offLast updated 21 November 2026

The 15 questions

  1. Are you licensed, and can I see the number?
  2. What background screening does every caregiver pass? (In Florida, Level 2 screening applies.)
  3. Are caregivers your employees, or contractors?
  4. What dementia training do they get, and how many hours?
  5. Will we have the same small team, and will we meet the backup?
  6. How fast can care start, and what is the record?
  7. What happens if a caregiver is sick or late?
  8. Who is our named contact, and how fast do they call back?
  9. How do you write and update the care plan?
  10. How will the family know what happened at each visit?
  11. What are the exact rates, minimum visit lengths and extra charges?
  12. What is the notice period to change or stop care?
  13. How do you handle falls, medication errors and complaints?
  14. Are you insured, including workers' compensation?
  15. Can we speak to families you currently care for?
An older woman reading papers in an armchair

Red flags

  • No license or registration number on the website
  • Vague prices or 'we will discuss it at the visit'
  • A different caregiver every week
  • Long lock-in contracts
  • Pressure to sign the same day
  • No written care plan
  • Reviews that only praise, with no replies to complaints
