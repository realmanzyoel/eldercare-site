The short answer

Wandering is common in dementia and can be dangerous. Reduce the reasons to leave (boredom, needing the toilet, looking for 'home'), make exits less obvious, use door alerts, keep a recent photo ready and agree an emergency plan with neighbours and family.

Why people wander

Walking often has a purpose: going to a job they used to have, looking for a parent, needing the toilet, or simply restlessness. Looking for the reason helps more than locks alone.

A home safety checklist

  • Door chimes or partner door sensors that alert the family's phones
  • A curtain or mural over the main door; a stop sign at eye level can help
  • Car keys out of sight
  • Daily walks with someone, so the need to move is met
  • Toilet signs and a night light on the route
  • ID bracelet or a card in their wallet with your number
  • A recent photo and a description of clothes, shared with family
A house with a porch and steps to the front door

If they go missing

Search the house and garden quickly, then call 911 within 15 minutes. Tell the police they have dementia. Check old routes: a former workplace, church, shops. Do not wait for hours.
